Kerberos is how AD proves who you are without sending your password across the
wire. It works with tickets issued by the Domain Controller. Understand the flow
and one of its design choices hands you a way to steal service account passwords.

## The ticket dance

Kerberos has three parties: the client, the **Key Distribution Center** (KDC,
which runs on the DC), and the service you want to reach. The flow:

- **AS-REQ / AS-REP**: the client proves it knows its password and receives a
  **Ticket Granting Ticket** (TGT), encrypted with the KDC's own key.
- **TGS-REQ / TGS-REP**: the client presents the TGT and asks for a ticket to a
  specific service. The KDC returns a **service ticket** (TGS).
- **AP-REQ**: the client presents the service ticket to the service, which trusts
  it because it is encrypted with the service account's password hash.

That last point is the whole game. A service ticket is encrypted with the hash of
the **service account's password**. If you can get a service ticket, you are
holding ciphertext that only that password can produce.

## SPNs: the name of a service

A **Service Principal Name** (SPN) links a service to the account that runs it,
in the form `MSSQLSvc/db01.corp.local:1433`. Any authenticated user can ask the
KDC for a service ticket to any SPN. The KDC does not check whether you are
actually allowed to use that service. It just hands over the ticket.

## Kerberoasting: crack the password offline

Put it together. As a normal user, you request service tickets for accounts that
have SPNs, extract the encrypted blob, and crack it offline at whatever speed
your hardware allows. No traffic to the DC during cracking, no lockouts, no alarms.

<CommandBlock
  title="attacker@kali"
  commands={[
    { cmd: "netexec ldap 10.10.10.5 -u jdoe -p Passw0rd --kerberoasting roast.txt", comment: "request tickets for all SPN accounts, save hashes" },
    { cmd: "hashcat -m 13100 roast.txt rockyou.txt", comment: "crack the TGS-REP hashes offline" },
  ]}
/>

<ExpectedOutput label="hashcat result">
$krb5tgs$23$*svc_sql$CORP.LOCAL$MSSQLSvc...:Summer2026!
Session..........: hashcat
Status...........: Cracked
</ExpectedOutput>

Service accounts are prime targets because they often have weak, rarely-rotated
passwords and sit in privileged groups.

<Callout type="security" title="Why this works so well">
Kerberoasting needs only one valid domain account and never touches the target
service. The crack happens entirely offline, so account lockout policies and
network monitoring do not slow you down. Strong service passwords and Group
Managed Service Accounts are the real defense.
</Callout>
