You cannot patch a person, but you can train, measure, and back them up. The goal
of defense is not a workforce that never gets fooled. That does not exist. The
goal is a workforce that notices, reports fast, and is caught by technical
controls when they slip. Done right, the human attack surface becomes a sensor
grid.

## Training that actually changes behavior

Annual click-through slides are compliance theater. Behavior changes when training
is frequent, specific, and tied to real feedback:

- **Simulated phishing**: send your own authorized test emails, then coach anyone
  who clicks in the moment. The teachable second is right after the click.
- **Role-relevant scenarios**: finance sees wire fraud, engineers see fake code
  reviews, HR sees fake resumes with macros.
- **Short and recurring**: a five minute nudge each month beats a two hour session
  once a year.
- **No blame**: shame makes people hide mistakes. You want them reported, not buried.

<Callout type="tip" title="Measure the report rate, not just the click rate">
Most programs only track who clicked. Track who reported instead. A rising report
rate means your users are becoming detectors. A team that clicks a little but
reports within two minutes is far safer than one that clicks nothing on the test
and stays silent on the real thing.
</Callout>

## A reporting culture

Speed of detection decides how bad an incident gets. A credential phished at 9:00
and reported at 9:03 can be contained before the attacker logs in. The same
credential reported next week is a breach. Make reporting the path of least
resistance:

- Give people a one-click **report phish** button in the mail client.
- Thank every report, even the false alarms. Punishing false positives kills the
  signal you need.
- Close the loop: tell reporters what happened so they see their report mattered.

## Technical controls that catch the slip

Awareness is one layer. Assume it fails sometimes and stack controls behind it:

- **Email authentication**: enforce SPF, DKIM, and DMARC so spoofed sender domains
  get rejected or quarantined.
- **Phishing-resistant MFA**: FIDO2 security keys do not hand a code to a fake
  page the way SMS codes do.
- **Least privilege**: a phished account that can reach little limits the blast.
- **Visitor and badge policy**: escort visitors, challenge tailgaters, and migrate
  off legacy prox cards.
- **DNS and web filtering**: block known malicious domains before the click lands.
