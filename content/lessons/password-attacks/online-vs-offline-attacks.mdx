There are two worlds of password attacks, and confusing them gets you caught or
locked out. **Online** attacks guess against a live service. **Offline** attacks
guess against a stolen hash on your own hardware. The rules, speed, and risks are
completely different.

## Online: guessing at the front door

An online attack sends candidates to a real login endpoint (SSH, RDP, a web form)
and reads the response. It is slow because it is limited by the network and the
server, and it is **loud**: every failed attempt is a log entry.

Worse, most services enforce **lockout**: too many failures and the account
freezes. This is why blasting one account with a huge wordlist is a beginner
mistake. You lock the account, alert the defender, and crack nothing.

## Spraying beats brute force online

**Password spraying** flips the strategy. Instead of many passwords against one
account, you try one common password against many accounts, then wait before the
next round. You stay under the lockout threshold on every individual account.

<CommandBlock
  title="attacker@kali"
  commands={[
    { cmd: "hydra -l admin -P rockyou.txt ssh://10.10.14.7", comment: "brute force one account, high lockout risk" },
    { cmd: "hydra -L users.txt -p 'Spring2024!' ssh://10.10.14.7", comment: "spray one password across many users, low risk" },
  ]}
/>

The difference is `-P` (a password list against one user) versus `-p` (one
password against a user list). That single flag is the line between reckless and
careful.

## Offline: the fast, silent world

If you can steal the hashes (a shadow file, a SAM dump, a database leak), the game
changes entirely. Now you crack on your own GPU with hashcat. There is no network
limit, no lockout, and no login log to trip. This is why grabbing hashes is such a
priority: it moves the fight to ground where you always win eventually.

<Callout type="info" title="The core tradeoff">
Online attacks are slow, loud, and lockout-limited, but they need no prior access.
Offline attacks are fast, silent, and unlimited, but you must first steal the
hashes. Most engagements go online only long enough to grab something you can
crack offline.
</Callout>

## Defenses that actually work

Know these so you can both attack and advise:

- **Lockout policies** blunt online guessing but do nothing offline.
- **Rate limiting and MFA** stop spraying cold.
- **Slow hashes** (bcrypt, argon2) with **per-user salts** make offline cracking
  expensive enough to abandon.
- **Long passphrases** beat every attack: length crushes both guessing and
  cracking budgets.

<Callout type="security" title="Why the hunter cares">
Read the target before you attack. A hardened login with MFA and lockout means
online guessing is a dead end, so pivot to capturing a hash. A weak-hashed dump
means the login barely matters. Match the attack to the terrain.
</Callout>
