Cracking is a guessing game, and a wordlist is your list of guesses. The quality
of the list decides whether you crack a hash in seconds or never. Raw brute force
(every possible character combination) is hopeless past a few characters, so real
attacks lean on human predictability instead.

## rockyou and friends

The most famous wordlist is `rockyou.txt`, roughly 14 million real passwords
leaked from a 2009 breach. It ships with Kali and lives under
`/usr/share/wordlists/`. Because it is made of passwords humans actually chose,
it cracks a shocking number of weak hashes on its own.

Beyond rockyou, the **SecLists** project bundles curated lists by category:
common passwords, default credentials, and leaked corporate sets. Bigger is not
always better. A focused list that matches your target beats a giant generic one.

## Build a custom list from target intel

Passwords reflect the people who make them. A company named Acme in Denver will
have accounts using `Acme2024`, `Denver!`, and `Broncos1`. Tools like `cewl`
scrape a target website and turn its words into a candidate list.

<CommandCard
  command="cewl -d 2 -m 5 -w custom.txt https://target.example"
  explain="Spider the site two links deep, keep words of 5+ characters, and write them to custom.txt as raw cracking fodder."
/>

## Rules: one word becomes hundreds

A raw word like `password` is rarely the actual password. People capitalize,
append years, and swap letters for symbols. **Rule-based mutation** applies these
transformations automatically, so one base word expands into many candidates:
`password`, `Password`, `Password1`, `P@ssw0rd`, `password2024`.

Hashcat ships rule files under `/usr/share/hashcat/rules/`. The `best64.rule` set
is the standard starting point: 64 high-value mutations that catch most human
habits without exploding your runtime.

<Callout type="tip" title="Rules beat raw size">
A 100,000-word list run through `best64.rule` tests 6.4 million candidates, and
they are smarter candidates than 6.4 million random strings. Reach for rules
before you reach for a bigger raw list.
</Callout>

<Callout type="security" title="Why the hunter cares">
The fastest crack on an engagement almost always comes from a small custom list
seeded with the target's own vocabulary (company name, products, local sports
teams) run through a rule set. Generic rockyou is your fallback, not your opener.
</Callout>
