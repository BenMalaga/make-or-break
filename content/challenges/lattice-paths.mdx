Picture a 20 by 20 grid of unit squares. A token starts at the **top-left**
corner of the grid and must reach the **bottom-right** corner. On every move it
may step only one square **right** or one square **down**. It never moves left,
up, or diagonally.

The question is a counting problem, not a search: **how many distinct routes**
reach the bottom-right corner?

Here is the key observation. To cross a 20 by 20 grid of squares you walk along
the grid lines, and any valid route is made of exactly 20 right moves and 20 down
moves, taken in some order. That is 40 moves total. A route is completely
determined the moment you decide which 20 of those 40 moves are the right moves,
because the rest must be down moves.

So the count is the number of ways to choose 20 positions out of 40, the binomial
coefficient C(40, 20). Do not try to enumerate the paths one by one. There are far
too many for a brute-force walk, but the combination formula gives the exact value
instantly.

In Python:

```python
import math
print(math.comb(40, 20))
```
