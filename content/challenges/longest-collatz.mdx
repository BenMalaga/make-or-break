The Collatz sequence starts from any positive whole number and repeatedly
applies one rule until it reaches 1:

- If the current number is even, the next number is half of it.
- If the current number is odd, the next number is three times it plus one.

For example, starting at 13 the chain runs:

```
13 -> 40 -> 20 -> 10 -> 5 -> 16 -> 8 -> 4 -> 2 -> 1
```

That chain has 10 terms. It is believed (though not proven) that every starting
number eventually reaches 1.

Your job: among all starting numbers **below 1,000,000**, find the one that
produces the **longest** chain. Submit that **starting number**, not the length
of its chain.

A naive approach recomputes the same tails millions of times and is far too slow.
The trick is memoization: keep a dictionary mapping a number to its chain length.
When you compute a new starting number, walk it forward until you hit a value
already in the dictionary, then fill in the length for every value you passed
through on the way. Each number gets its length computed exactly once, which makes
the full scan fast.
