A **JSON Web Token** (JWT) is what a lot of sites hand your browser after you log
in. It looks like one long random blob, so people assume it is encrypted and safe
to stuff secrets into. It is not. A JWT is **signed, not encrypted**. The
signature only proves the token was not tampered with. It does nothing to hide
what is inside.

A JWT has three parts separated by dots:

```
header.payload.signature
```

Each part is **base64url**, a URL-safe variant of base64 (it uses `-` and `_`
instead of `+` and `/`, and often drops the `=` padding). Anyone holding the
token can decode the payload and read every claim in it, no key required.

Here is a token. Someone dropped a `flag` claim into its payload:

```
eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJzdWIiOiIxMzM3IiwibmFtZSI6Im1vYi1sZWFybmVyIiwicm9sZSI6InN0dWRlbnQiLCJmbGFnIjoiTU9Ce2p3dF9pc19zaWduZWRfbm90X3NlY3JldH0ifQ.not-a-real-signature-decode-the-middle-part
```

Split it on the dots, take the middle part (the payload), base64url-decode it to
JSON, and read the value of the `flag` claim. The signature is a placeholder and
is not checked here. Run the starter code, or decode it however you like, then
submit the flag exactly as it reads, including the `MOB{...}` wrapper.
