## Factorial Digit Sum

The factorial of a number `n`, written `n!`, is the product of every integer
from 1 up to `n`. For example, `5! = 5 * 4 * 3 * 2 * 1 = 120`.

Factorials grow fast. `100!` is a 158-digit whole number, far beyond what a
64-bit integer or a float can hold. Python handles this for you: its built-in
integers are arbitrary precision, so `100!` is stored exactly.

Your task:

1. Compute `100!` exactly.
2. Sum all of its decimal digits. For instance, if the number were `120`, the
   digit sum would be `1 + 2 + 0 = 3`.

Run your code, read the total, and submit that single number.

Watch out for one trap: do not convert the factorial to a float anywhere. A
float cannot represent a 158-digit integer exactly, and you would sum the digits
of an approximation. Keep everything as an integer or a string.
